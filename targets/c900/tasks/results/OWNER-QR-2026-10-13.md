# Owner observation: QR scan in Hiwear Plus (2026-10-13)

Source: owner report with four photos/screenshots (not committed: they show the owner's name, the watch QR and part of the address).

## Observed [H, owner report]
- After unbinding in the app and removing the Bluetooth pairing, the watch shows a QR code titled "Hiwear Plus".
- In Hiwear Plus, "Scan code" on that QR connects at once ("Connecting", then "Watch Ultra | <last two octets>", Connected); phone notifications appear on the watch right away.
- No separate Bluetooth pairing step was needed by the owner.

## What this means
| Claim | Tag | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The QR scan is the vendor app's way in (its device picker) | [H] owner | App UX |
| The QR is needed by the protocol | **no** [H] | LIVE-SESSION-1: the probe connected, read GATT and subscribed without any QR. C900-Q19: no SMP, no encryption, no key exchange on LE |
| What the QR encodes | [U], likely the watch MAC (the app labels the device with the last two octets) [I] | Do not decode the photo into the repo: it holds the address. Static check S-22: smali of the scan-code screen to see which QR fields the app uses (MAC only, or also a name/key) |
| What actually binds | [C]+[H] | cmd 3 key 0 with the app's 8-byte id, watch answers 0x0C key 8 (C900-Q19) |

## Consequence for the open client
The client needs a way to pick the watch (scan list by name `Watch Ultra` + MAC-plus-`12 34` scan record [H], or optionally a QR reader that extracts the address). QR is a convenience, not a security step, unless S-22 finds a key in it.
